What this models. Gate-by-gate evaluation over all 23 input vectors: p = a ^ b, g = a & b, t = p & cin, r = b & cin, sum = p ^ cin, cout = g | t | r. A planted fault forces one net to a constant; everything downstream sees the forced value. A test "fails" on a vector when any watched signal differs from the good part.

The survivor, named. cout needs only g | t; the r = b & cin term is redundant cover — belt-and-braces a review waved through. Wherever r matters, g | t already answers, so r stuck-at-0 changes no output on any vector: activated 2 of 8, observed 0, forever. Measured on this netlist: watching sum alone can ever catch 10 of 18 faults; watching both pins, 17 of 18; one tap on r closes the last — 2 of 8 vectors show it there. Redundant logic is where stuck-ats go to hide, which is why synthesis tools hunt redundancy and why fault-model people care.

Same axis, one level up. The lecture's addc.sv run (just demo 01 s1 2) drives 65,536 pairs: wrong on 128, observed at sum 0 times, at carry_out 128 times. There the wrong value reached an unwatched pin; here it never reaches a pin at all. Scan chains and a controllability/observability testability score exist to buy back exactly this visibility — in a testbench, the tap is one added assert on an internal net.
