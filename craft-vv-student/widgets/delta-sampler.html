Delta Sampler

One register — always @(posedge clk) q <= d; — and a probe you drag along the timeline. The waveform draws a clock edge as a single vertical line. It is not: drag the probe onto an edge and the line opens into the settle steps the simulator runs at that instant. Where your await lands among them decides what your read returns.

Your test wakes at the t=20 ns edge and reads on the next Python line:
await RisingEdge(dut.clk)      # the t=20 ns edge
got = int(dut.q.value)         # next line — same simulated instant
d has been 0x3C since t=13 ns; q still holds 0xA5 from the edge before. What does the read return?
Commit to an answer — the probe readings and the comparison unlock once you do. You can drag the probe first to see where the edge opens up.

Verdict

Waiting for your prediction.

What this models. cocotb 1.x on the course toolchain (Icarus). Inside one edge instant, in order: the clk value-change callback resumes every coroutine awaiting RisingEdge — the writable phase, where your drives belong; the RTL always block runs and schedules q <= d; nonblocking updates apply and q changes; cocotb deposits (dut.x.value = ...) land in the read-write phase; ReadOnly fires last, when every delta cycle for the step is done. A drive in ReadOnly raises; await NextTimeStep() is the exit. A Timer wake at an instant runs before that instant's deposits land, which is why a read at exactly edge+1 ns misses a redrive at exactly edge+1 ns.

The rule that generalizes: drive in the writable phase, sample in ReadOnly(). The LRM gives no contract for a read in the writable phase — on this toolchain it returns the pre-update value every run, and a habit built on that accident breaks on the next simulator.
