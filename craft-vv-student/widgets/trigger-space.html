The other tool. The same safety contract — assert ((grant & ~req) == 0) — through SymbiYosys: the trojan task FAILs at step 1 and hands back the exact trigger, req = 4'b1010 → grant = 4'b0100; the good task PASSes by k-induction — absence for every input, not "none found". Measured on this repo: every run lands in under a second. just demo 08 s2 1

What this models. One uniform random vector per cycle; a hit is landing in the trigger region — T of 2b patterns, so P(hit)/vector = T/2b and P(hit within N vectors) = 1 − (1 − T/2b)N. The arbiter of dut/arbiter_trojan.sv is the b=4, T=1 case: wire magic = (N == 4) && (req == 4'b1010); assign grant = magic ? 4'b0100 : grant_rr;. Production backdoors differ by one number — the width of the compare. Sampling is with replacement, as random stimulus is; even a perfect no-repeat enumerator needs 2b/2T tries on average, the same order. A k-cycle arming sequence multiplies the space again: the region then lives in (2b)k.

The default rate is measured, not guessed: the Week 5 demo's captured cocotb run advances 15,077.52 ns of simulated time per wall-clock second — about 1,500 cycles/s at its 10 ns clock.
